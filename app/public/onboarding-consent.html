Before we test anything, here is exactly what happens.

Read it once. Nothing runs and nothing is stored until you agree.

What Wi-Fight collects
Only what the speed check needs
Speed test results
Download, upload, and response time for each test.
Each test measures three things: how fast data comes down, how fast it goes up, and how quickly the connection answers. Wi-Fight keeps these numbers on this device, encrypted, so it can work out your daily middle value.
The time of each test
So we can see if your speed drops at busy hours.
Wi-Fight saves the date and time of each test. That is how it can tell a slow evening from a slow whole day. It does not record what you were doing at that time.
Your provider, plan, and monthly price
So we can compare what you get with what you pay for.
You pick your plan from a list or type it in. Wi-Fight uses the speed on the plan label and the monthly price to work out your fair line and what a shortfall costs you.
A photo of your bill, only while it is read
If you scan a bill, the photo is read once, then it is deleted.
If you choose to scan a bill, the photo is held in this page only while it is read. Only the prices and fee names you confirm are saved. The photo itself is never written to storage.
What Wi-Fight never collects
We do not ask for it and we cannot see it
The sites you visit
We measure speed. We never look at what you do online.
A speed test sends and receives test data only. Wi-Fight has no way to see your browsing, your apps, or anything else on your network.
Your home address
We do not ask where you live.
Wi-Fight never asks for a street address or uses your location. Note that the IP address M-Lab publishes can show your provider and rough area, which is why we ask you first.
Your passwords or account logins
Not for your provider, not for anything else.
Sign-in uses a one-time code or a passkey, so there is no password. Wi-Fight never asks for your provider account login.
One thing you should know
Public record

Speed tests run on M-Lab, an open research platform. M-Lab publishes each test's results, time, and IP address, and keeps them. That is how open internet research works, and it is why we ask first.
